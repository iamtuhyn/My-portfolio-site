Designing a habit, not an app.

A one-line brief became a five-role product with a reason to come back.

Problem

Recycling apps lose people after the first return. For Recyclium that was a business risk: every role downstream depends on consumers coming back.

What I did

Defined five roles and their handoffs from a one-line brief, designed the consumer journey from a QR scan to long-term participation, and a progression system that makes each return worth more.

Result

A complete 0 → 1 product across five roles and two platforms, delivered as prototypes to engineering.

01

The brief was a sentence

"Create a connected recycling platform to track and manage discarded items, streamline returns and logistics, confirm recycling, and reward participants."

No spec, no legacy product, no defined flow. Before opening Figma we spent time on a whiteboard, turning that sentence into objectives we could design against: a closed loop where every item is traceable, easy returns, collection and logistics, recycling confirmation recorded on-chain, impact reporting, and rewards for every participant.

02

Five roles, one loop

Producers → Consumers → Storers → Recyclers → Waste managers

Each role has different goals, permissions and data. I mapped the handoff points first, because every later screen depends on who holds the item, who confirms it, and who gets paid.

Our focus was the consumer: the entry point for all downstream data, and the least patient user. They don't know what a blockchain is and don't want to learn. They need to understand "what's in it for me" within 30 seconds.

The real problem: the second session

How do we make recycling feel like a habit, not a chore?

Curiosity and goodwill get the first return. What's missing is a structural reason to come back. If people don't return, the whole ecosystem has nothing to process.

03

Earn first, sign up second

Scan product QR → reward eligibility → claim → sign up → mission → scan items → aggregated QR → hand over → rate & earn → points & vouchers → share

04

Six decisions that mattered

01

The product is the front door

The journey starts by scanning a QR code on a real product. You see the reward before you're asked for anything.

02

One aggregated QR at handover

Collected items are bundled into one code. The storer scans once, and blockchain logging stays invisible to the consumer.

03

Progression that compounds

Tiers raise the value of future recycling: Eco Newbie 1.0×, Waste Warrior 1.1×, Planet Protector 1.25×. Streaks and badges add short-term goals.

04

Points you can feel

Points show an approximate cash value before they can be redeemed. Claim vouchers, track expiry, and send points to friends by QR.

05

A path to a bigger role

Engaged consumers can become storers (collection points) through a guided request and verification.

06

Leaderboards on both sides

Separate rankings for consumers and storers: progress and community, without comparing unlike roles.

08

What it taught me

5stakeholder roles, with separate flows and permissions
2platforms: mobile PWA and web dashboards
0 → 1the full consumer lifecycle, from first scan to redemption
Motivation design comes before interaction design.

Before drawing a screen, you have to know why someone comes back tomorrow. What I'd do differently:

  • Test whether people understand the reward earlier. Do users grasp what their points are worth before they can redeem them? If not, motivation drops at exactly the wrong moment.
  • Prompt the storer upgrade at the right moment. Today people have to discover it; a nudge at an engagement milestone would speed it up.
