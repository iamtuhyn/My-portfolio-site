Problem

Dozens of subcontractors, one gate, a few cranes, and deliveries whenever they liked. Coordination ran on calls and spreadsheets.

What I did

Led UX across five product surfaces, connecting site planning, construction logistics, material handling and building move-in. I worked on the information architecture, core workflows, shared components and mobile experiences for teams operating on-site.

Result

Established a consistent design language and restructured the information architecture across five product surfaces, supporting connected workflows from site planning to daily logistics and move-in operations.

08 · Decisions

Four decisions
that mattered

01

Separate "now" from "how are we doing"

The Cockpit is live: what's at the gate, what's late. The Dashboard is analytical: completion rate, arrival accuracy, lead times.

02

The booking is the core object

Every shipment ties to a slot, a zone and equipment, with one status model everywhere: open, confirmed, completed, rejected, express, ad-hoc.

03

Shared equipment, shown as shared

Cranes and forklifts are booked like rooms, with booked vs. used hours side by side, so overruns are visible instead of argued about.

04

Make every number explainable

A KPI glossary: every indicator with its definition, formula, source and reconciliation rule. Designed in about 30 minutes with AI, reviewed line by line.

Why it mattersSite managers didn't trust totals they couldn't reconcile. Now every number explains itself, including why totals don't always add up.
